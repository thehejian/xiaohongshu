Ink wash painting style, light rice paper texture, flowing ink strokes, vivid crimson and grey colours, dramatic composition, misty atmosphere. A dramatic scene inside the great hall of the Han imperial court: a young emperor of only fourteen, small for his age, sits upright on a low throne behind a wide lacquer desk (an, the Han-dynasty low table), dressed in authentic Western Han imperial ceremonial robes — a black (xuan) silk upper garment with wide sleeves over a deep crimson lower garment, a tall black silk crown (tongtianguan, flat-topped court hat) atop his head, calm and confident. One hand holds an open silk scroll letter, the other points decisively toward the court below. Before him, a great general in dark robes kneels bare-headed, removing his cap in shame, while rows of startled Han officials in black-and-crimson court robes gasp and exchange shocked glances. Guards with halberds stand in formation, bronze lamps and hanging silk curtains. The moment a teenage emperor sees through a forged letter that fooled the court, dramatic tension, candlelight and shadow. Chinese title "少年识诈" in large bold calligraphic brush style.
